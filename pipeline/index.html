From a target to a cited dossier

A complete computational drug-discovery pipeline, described by what it does — not by the machinery underneath.

Research use only. NEXUS is a computational research platform. It is not clinically validated, has no regulatory approval, and does not provide diagnosis, treatment or clinical advice. Every output requires independent experimental and clinical validation.
Stage 01

Intake and identity

Start with a protein target, a UniProt accession, a sequence, a gene, or a disease. NEXUS resolves the identity against public reference data, records what it knows, and fails loudly when an identifier cannot be resolved rather than guessing.

  • Target and sequence resolution
  • Gene, disease and organism context
  • Structured research record
Stage 02

Evidence assembly

The pipeline gathers public evidence from biomedical databases and registries, then binds each statement to a source and an evidence tier. The result is an evidence chain, not an unsourced summary.

  • Disease, gene, structure and trial evidence
  • Source identifiers and retrieval dates
  • Evidence tiers and explicit gaps
Stage 03

Structural assessment

Where structural data exist, NEXUS uses them to ask better questions about domains, pockets, interfaces and stability. Experimental structures and computational models are labelled separately. A structure is treated as evidence, not as proof.

  • Experimental and predicted structures
  • Domain and pocket context
  • Confidence and limitations recorded
Stage 04

Mechanism and modality

Rare disease is not only a protein problem. The pipeline assesses the mechanism and the appropriate route: small molecule, biologic, gene therapy, editing, RNA-directed approach, protein replacement, cell therapy, or symptomatic care.

  • Mechanism-aware route selection
  • Multiple therapeutic modalities
  • Explicit “research needed” where evidence is thin
Stage 05

Therapeutic design

Where the mechanism supports a design, NEXUS generates candidate routes and records the reasoning, the target engagement hypothesis and the proposed readout. Designs are research hypotheses, not medicines.

  • Candidate therapeutic routes
  • Target engagement and safety hypotheses
  • Falsifiers and critical experiments
Stage 06

Safety and evidence gates

Before anything leaves the research environment, it passes through evidence and safety checks: unresolved citations, unsupported claims, single-run results presented as conclusions, and clinical overreach are rejected by design.

  • Citation and source checks
  • Benchmark leakage and result gates
  • Scope and compassion review
Stage 07

Dossier and scientist review

The output is a cited dossier: the disease, the gene, the evidence, the hypotheses, the candidate routes, the trials, the gaps and the next experiment. The scientist reviews it, challenges it, and owns the judgement.

  • Citation-referenced research dossier
  • Gaps and review questions
  • Read-only trust boundary
Stage 08

Portfolio scale

The same pipeline can run across a portfolio. For the orphan-disease programme, NEXUS catalogued 11,645 clinical entities, ran the therapeutic stage on 4,245 gene-associated entities, and produced 15 enriched priority dossiers. Where the record had no gene, it said so.

  • Portfolio-level evidence status
  • Explicit no-gene records
  • No invented targets

What the pipeline gives a scientist

A structured starting point: what is known, what is uncertain, what is missing, which hypotheses are testable, and which experiment would move the field. The pipeline organises evidence and designs; the scientist sets the question and validates the answer.
